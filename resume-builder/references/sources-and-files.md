# Sources and files: only what the person gave you

Everything in a document must come from one of three places: the person's **answers**, **files** they send, or **postings and notes they paste**. Claude's own knowledge, guesses and web searches are not sources for facts. This protects the person in interviews: they can explain every line because it came from them.

## Files people can send
Invite them at the start (and again in Q3). Good files:
- Old resume or CV (PDF, Word, text)
- LinkedIn profile text or export
- Job postings (text, PDF or screenshot)
- Transcripts, course lists, certificates, licences (with dates)
- Project write-ups, READMEs, reports, slide decks, portfolio pages
- Performance reviews, thank-you notes, reports or dashboards that contain real numbers
- Company text they copied (About page, news they found) for a cover letter

Do **not** ask for or accept: passwords, ID or passport numbers, social insurance or security numbers, bank details, full home address, or health information. If a file contains these, tell the person to remove them (or ignore and never copy them into any document).

## How to use a file
1. Read it. Copy only relevant facts into `profile.md`, each tagged with its source, for example `(source: old-resume.pdf)` or `(source: answer Q10)`.
2. Anything you inferred rather than read directly gets `CONFIRM`. Ask the person before it goes on a document.
3. Ask only what the files do not answer. If two sources disagree (dates, titles, numbers), do not pick one: ask.
4. Put copies in `sources/` inside the person's working folder and never send them anywhere.
5. Say what you used: "I took your job dates and the 800 to 1,300 follower numbers from your old resume."

## What Claude may not do
- Search the web, or look anything up, unless the person explicitly asks for it. If they ask, mark every fact from outside as `researched: <URL, date>` and tell them to verify it before sending.
- Add "typical" duties, industry statistics, example achievements or skills that are "usually expected" for a role.
- Describe a company beyond what the posting or the person's pasted text says.
- Fill gaps with plausible-sounding detail. A gap is a question, not an opening for guessing.

## What Claude may do
- Improve wording, order and layout of the person's facts.
- Point out gaps and ask for the missing fact or number.
- Explain a concept from the posting if the person asks, labeled as explanation, never as a claim about them.
- Check a posting link the person gave, only to read the text (and only if they asked or agreed).
